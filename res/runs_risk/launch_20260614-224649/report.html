geneal — risk-aware target nomination

40 cell lines · 6 seeds · K=30 targets/portfolio
Efficacy-only nomination puts 45% of the portfolio in one pathway and carries toxicity 0.20. The selective+hedged strategy (constrained_cap3) cuts toxicity to 0.03 and concentration to 29%, while keeping max-efficacy 0.89 vs 1.85 (mean efficacy 0.35→0.07).

How to read this

Strategies
• efficacy — pick the K targets with highest predicted lethality, NO safety constraint. The naive baseline (“maximize efficacy, check safety later”).
• constrained — maximize predicted lethality SUBJECT TO toxicity ≤ τ (a hard safety ceiling). Toxicity = common-essential score (known pan-cancer annotation); efficacy in this line is predicted.
• constrained_cap<c> — constrained, but allow at most c genes per pathway/complex (e.g. cap2 = max 2 per pathway). Adds the portfolio hedge.

Why a constraint, not a weighted penalty? Safety is usually a bar, not a tradeable quantity — a too-toxic target is disqualified. Why also hedge pathways? If many targets sit in one pathway and it proves toxic/undruggable, the whole bet fails together; capping spreads it across independent mechanisms.
