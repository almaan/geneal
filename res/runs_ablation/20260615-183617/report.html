geneal — default ablation (safety + diversity)

5113 genes (data/processed/depmap/panel_5k.txt) · 6 cell lines · 3 seeds · K=30 targets · AL 8×10 · τ=0.5 · cap=2

How to read this

Two separate questions, two analyses. We do NOT entangle safety and diversity into one grid.

Analysis A — safety vs efficacy. One axis, the safety rule applied when nominating the final K targets:
• greedy — top-K by predicted lethality, NO safety constraint (naive baseline).
• truncation — max predicted lethality s.t. known toxicity ≤ τ (common-essential ceiling, a priori).
• ehvi — a dual-objective active-learning acquisition (Expected Hypervolume Improvement over efficacy+toxicity GPs) that learns toxicity; nomination filters on the predicted-toxicity ceiling.
• random / coreset / typiclust — naive + prior-work (IterPert-style) acquisition baselines.

Analysis B — diversity / robustness. Operators applied at nomination, layered on a base:
• none — top-K by quality (concentration-blind).
• cap — at most c genes per CORUM pathway/complex (hard hedge; bolts onto any method).
• kdpp — quality-weighted k-DPP with a mechanism similarity S (soft diversity). S is dense PubMedBERT-embedding cosine by default (tunable), or sparse STRING; never outcome similarity — we want the picks to share the high-efficacy outcome, so diversity must live in mechanism space.

Representations. PubMedBERT embeddings predict efficacy/toxicity and supply the dense k-DPP similarity (two uses of one fingerprint). STRING (a network → a similarity) and CORUM (pathway membership) are not per-gene feature vectors, so they only structure the diversity step; they never predict.
