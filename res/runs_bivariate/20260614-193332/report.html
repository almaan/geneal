Learning toxicity (vs knowing it a priori) recovers 113% of the oracle hypervolume on the contrast reference by round 5 — the cost of joint efficacy-toxicity exploration.

How to read this

Setup. Both efficacy (lethality in the target line) and toxicity are unknown and explored jointly by an active-learning loop using EHVI (Expected Hypervolume Improvement) — each round acquires the batch expected to most expand the dominated region of the (efficacy↑, toxicity↓) objective space.
Toxicity reference — contrast: lethality in a fixed proxy cell line (a different-domain stand-in for normal tissue); population: mean lethality across all cell lines.
Regime — learned: toxicity is predicted by a 2nd surrogate and explored; known: toxicity is an oracle (known a priori) — the ceiling. The learned-vs-known gap = the cost of not knowing toxicity up front.
Hypervolume: dominated area of the revealed set in objective space — higher = better coverage of high-efficacy / low-toxicity targets.
